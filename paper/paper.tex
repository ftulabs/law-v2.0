\begin{abstract}
Regulatory indices are compiled by hand: an analyst is given a jurisdiction and
a legal question, searches official portals, reads statutes, and records which
article answers it. Automating this is usually framed as a retrieval problem
over a corpus that has already been assembled. We argue the corpus is the
problem. We present VeriTrade, an end-to-end harness that is given a
jurisdiction and a regulatory topic and \emph{no source URLs}, and which
discovers statutes on official portals, acquires them against bot defences and
encrypted catalogues, recovers text from scanned images, segments instruments
into articles, retrieves candidate provisions with a script-aware hybrid ranker,
maps each to a codified legal test with a model that may never author a
citation, and emits a confidence with explicit routing to human review. It runs
across six Asia-Pacific economies in three writing systems. Our central finding
is that the failures introduced by this setting are silent: at every stage a
plausible implementation returns a confident, empty answer that is
indistinguishable from a jurisdiction with no such law. We demonstrate one such
failure under controlled conditions. \claim{C1} Both fixes are necessary and
neither is sufficient, and in three of the four configurations nothing in the
pipeline raises, logs, or exits non-zero. \claim{C2} Every number in this paper
is re-derived from run records at compile time.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Regional Digital Trade Integration Index scores an economy's digital-trade
regulation across twelve thematic pillars resolving to sixty-one legal
questions \citep{escap2024rdtii}. Each question is answered not with a
judgement but with an instrument, an article within it, and that article's text
quoted verbatim. Coding one economy today is a person reading statutes.

Automating this looks like retrieval, and the field has excellent benchmarks for
it. COLIEE has run statute and case-law retrieval for over a decade
\citep{goebel2023coliee}; LexGLUE assembles seven legal understanding tasks
\citep{chalkidis2022lexglue}; LegalBench extends to legal reasoning
\citep{guha2023legalbench}; BSARD poses exactly our question shape --- natural
language in, statutory article out \citep{louis2022bsard}. In every one of
them the corpus is handed to the system.

That is the right design for measuring a model, and it is why none of them can
see the failures this paper is about. On the real task the corpus does not exist
until the system builds it, and building it is where the difficulty is
concentrated. Meanwhile the deployed systems that do attempt legal research are
measurably unreliable: an audit of commercial tools from LexisNexis and Thomson
Reuters, marketed on their freedom from hallucination, found fabricated or
misattributed authority between roughly one in six and one in three responses
\citep{magesh2024hallucination}, with higher rates on open-ended case questions
\citep{dahl2024fictions}.

We take the constraint that follows as architectural rather than statistical.
The model in our pipeline grades evidence; it never writes it. Law text,
article numbers and URLs are carried forward from extraction, and the one field
the model may propose --- a subsection reference --- is discarded unless it
occurs literally in the retrieved snippet.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An end-to-end harness for autonomous legal evidence retrieval.}
Seven stages from a bare jurisdiction-plus-topic input to cited, confidence-scored
indicator rows, with no seed URLs at any point (Section~\ref{sec:method}). To our
knowledge no prior published system combines autonomous source discovery with
extraction and indicator mapping in one evaluated pipeline; we state that as a
conjunction and discuss the closest prior work in Section~\ref{sec:related}.
\item \textbf{A script-aware hybrid retrieval design}, and the measured
consequences of each of its parts, including two counter-intuitive negative
results that we record so they are not re-proposed (Section~\ref{sec:retrieval},
Section~\ref{sec:sweep}).
\item \textbf{A characterisation of silent failure} as the dominant failure mode
of this task, with a controlled demonstration on six economies and three writing
systems (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
\item \textbf{A provenance harness.} Every number in this paper is re-derived
from run records at build time, and the document does not compile if one stops
resolving (Section~\ref{sec:provenance}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Legal benchmarks assume the corpus.} COLIEE distributes a fixed Civil
Code article set \citep{goebel2023coliee}; LexGLUE and LegalBench evaluate
understanding and reasoning over supplied text
\citep{chalkidis2022lexglue,guha2023legalbench}; BSARD pairs questions with
22{,}600 pre-segmented Belgian articles \citep{louis2022bsard}. A benchmark whose
corpus is given cannot expose a discovery or acquisition failure, because it has
already paid the cost that dominates the task. A January 2026 survey of legal
LLM agents \citep{liu2026legalagents} covers compliance and regulatory
applications and describes no system that locates legal sources without seed
URLs or a pre-built collection; its own framing is that agents delegate authority
to legal databases and APIs, which assumes discovery away.

\paragraph{Acquisition at scale.} Pile of Law collects 256\,GB of US legal
material from a known, enumerated set of sources \citep{henderson2022pileoflaw}.
Closest to us is LOCUS \citep{peskoff2026locus}, which collects a US local
ordinance corpus using browser automation against anti-bot defences, OCRs it,
segments it into individual laws and classifies each segment --- four of our
seven stages. It differs in that its targets are a known set of commercial
code-hosting vendors rather than portals to be discovered, it covers one country
and one language, and it reports no confidence calibration or review routing.
Our differentiator against LOCUS is discovery, multi-script breadth, and
calibrated routing --- not OCR and segmentation.

\paragraph{Structured regulatory extraction.} REGREACT extracts typed compliance
criteria from regulation-length documents \citep{ali2026regreact}, and a
multi-jurisdictional RAG system covers 242 documents across 68 jurisdictions
\citep{ford2026multijurisdictional}. Both operate on document sets supplied as
input. Generative-search audits show that fluent responses routinely carry
unsupported or inaccurate citations \citep{liu2023verifiability}; our response is
to make the citation a retrieval artifact the model cannot write.

\paragraph{Retrieval.} We combine BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25} with a dense
bi-encoder \citep{karpukhin2020dpr} and a cross-encoder reranker
\citep{nogueira2020monot5}, fused by convex combination rather than reciprocal
rank fusion \citep{cormack2009rrf}; \citet{bruch2022fusion} analyse that choice
and the score normalisation it requires. Multilingual retrieval is established
by Mr.\ TyDi and MIRACL \citep{zhang2021mrtydi,zhang2023miracl}, but both
evaluate \emph{per language} on a per-language index. The interference we report
in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} arises only when several scripts share one index.
\citet{ogundepo2022whitespace} show character $n$-grams substitute well for
language-specific tokenizers in BM25; we take that as given and ask a different
question --- what a deployed system \emph{reports} when it has the wrong one.

\paragraph{Documents and calibration.} OCR error degrades downstream NLP
measurably \citep{vanstrien2020ocr}, which is why we treat recognition quality as
a first-class variable. \citet{aumiller2021segmentation} segment legal documents
by topical coherence; we must instead recover formal article boundaries across
three drafting traditions. For routing, \citet{kamath2020selective} show that
softmax confidence fails under domain shift --- directly load-bearing for a
system crossing six jurisdictions. Our per-provision provenance record is a
datasheet \citep{gebru2021datasheets} pushed down to the individual article.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Given a jurisdiction $j$ and indicators $I=\{i_1,\dots,i_k\}$, each carrying a
codified legal test, the system must return tuples
$\langle\,\text{instrument},\,\text{article},\,\text{verbatim snippet},\,
\text{source URL},\,\text{confidence}\,\rangle$ such that the snippet occurs
character-for-character in the document at that URL and the article's operative
rule satisfies $i$'s test. Where no provision satisfies $i$ the system must say
so explicitly and name where it looked: a blank is indistinguishable from a
failure to search. Three constraints separate this from a retrieval benchmark.
\textbf{No seed URLs} --- the system is told which official portals exist and
nothing else; no document URL or instrument name may be embedded.
\textbf{Scanned sources count} --- some instruments are published only as page
images. \textbf{The answer may not be precomputed} --- evaluation is a live run.

The difficulty concentrates in four places, and each is a place where a plausible
implementation returns a confident empty answer. Statutes sit behind portal
software written for human readers, so general web search surfaces commentary
more readily than the statute. Portals resist automated collection. An
instrument is not an answer: returning a 618-article Act restates the question,
so segmentation must be reliable enough that the citation is checkable.
Finally, neighbouring indicators share their vocabulary --- a prohibition on
transferring data abroad and a permission to transfer it subject to safeguards
are written in almost the same words, and retrieval correctly ranks both highly.

\subsection{Discovery without seed URLs}
\label{sec:discover}

The system is configured with one domain per economy and no document URLs.
Queries are constructed from the indicators themselves, and split by sub-type:
short law-title fragments, and descriptive obligation phrases. The distinction
is operational --- Australia's register API matches against the Act title only,
so obligation phrases fired at it can never return a hit and merely spend the
query budget. Per-indicator terms are interleaved round-robin, so that
truncating the query list starves no indicator.

Portals then need portal-specific mechanisms, which we summarise here and detail
in Appendix~\ref{app:portals}. Malaysia serves its catalogue as an AES-GCM
payload whose key the page publishes for its own JavaScript; we decrypt exactly
as the page does. Singapore's browse listing ignores its pagination parameter
entirely, so the index is enumerated by unioning four sort windows. Australia
exposes an OData register. India publishes one record per \emph{section} through
a DSpace API, so provision boundaries are the publisher's own. Mongolia injects
its body client-side, so the document is reached through the export endpoint its
own toolbar calls.

\subsection{Acquisition}
\label{sec:acquire}

Fetching escalates from a browser-impersonating HTTP client to a stealth browser
only when a page proves to be an unrendered application shell. Bodies are
content-addressed by SHA-256, so two URLs serving identical bytes occupy one
file, and conditional requests avoid refetching.

Two decisions are worth stating. First, \texttt{robots.txt} is enforced with
unknown treated as denial: a server that cannot tell us its rules has not granted
permission. Second, where a portal states that its documents are not to be
downloaded --- China's national database returns an explicit
\texttt{download:\,0} permission block --- we stop rather than engineer around
it, and use a different official portal that publishes the same national laws as
ordinary pages. We record this because it is a design constraint with a coverage
cost, not a limitation we discovered late.

\subsection{Recognition and segmentation}
\label{sec:ocr}

Documents are triaged per page, not per file, because a file can carry a text
layer on some pages and images on others. Pages with a usable text layer are
read directly; the rest are rendered at 300\,dpi and passed to OCR. Recognition
language is selected per economy from a registry that records, for each engine,
whether a model for that script exists at all --- and an engine with no model is
not invoked. This matters because an engine can only emit characters in its
output dictionary: an audit of the shipped recognition dictionaries found the
Cyrillic model missing four Mongolian letters and sixteen of Kazakh's, and the
Latin model missing all forty-five precomposed Vietnamese tone forms. A missing
letter is not a low score; it is an impossibility that arrives as fluent text.

Segmentation recovers article boundaries per drafting tradition. Singapore and
Malaysia number sections; Australia typesets them bold with no keyword; China
uses an article marker that PDF extraction routinely splits with spaces; Mongolia numbers articles in laws but uses a
decimal clause style in ministerial circulars. Snippets are preserved verbatim:
page and heading sentinels are stripped from the snippet but retained in the
source text, so a provision spanning a page break still verifies exactly.

\subsection{Hybrid retrieval}
\label{sec:retrieval}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\resizebox{0.92\linewidth}{!}{\input{fig_retrieval.tex}}
\caption{The retrieval stage. Retrieval is indicator-conditioned: the ranker runs
once per indicator over the economy's whole provision set.}
\label{fig:retrieval}
\end{figure}

Retrieval (Figure~\ref{fig:retrieval}) scores every provision against every
indicator. For provision $i$ and indicator $q$ the fused score is
\[
  s_i \;=\; \tfrac12\Big[\max\big(0,\;
      \alpha\,\tfrac{b_i}{\max_j b_j}
      + (1-\alpha)\,\tfrac{\cos_i + 1}{2}
      + \beta_i - \pi_i \big)\Big] \;+\; \tfrac12\,\sigma(c_i),
\]
with $\alpha=0.65$, $b$ the BM25 score max-normalised over the corpus, $\cos$ the
cosine of a multilingual bi-encoder mapped to $[0,1]$, $\sigma$ the logistic, and
$c$ the cross-encoder logit, defined as zero outside the rerank pool.

Three design choices carry the multi-script setting.

\paragraph{Tokenisation is script-aware, and the ASCII path is invariant.} A run
in a script without word spacing becomes overlapping character bigrams; spaced
scripts tokenise as words; accented Latin shares a character class with ASCII
rather than forming its own. The last point is not cosmetic: giving diacritics
their own class shreds Vietnamese into one-letter fragments that match any
document, returning plausible non-zero scores computed from noise. Critically,
pure-ASCII input tokenises bit-identically to the original English-only rule ---
pinned by regression test --- which is what licenses transferring parameters
swept on English corpora to the multilingual system.

\paragraph{Lexical and dense stages see different text.} The BM25 corpus includes
the law name for document-level discrimination through IDF; the embedded text
excludes it, because the law name otherwise dominates the embedding and makes
every section of a data-protection act look like a framework hit. Native-language
query terms are added to the lexical query only: the bi-encoder is cross-lingual
by construction, so an English question already reaches foreign text, while BM25
without native terms scores a flat zero.

\paragraph{The reranker is disabled rather than substituted.} The shipped
cross-encoder is English-only, and its score is fused at the same weight as
BM25 --- so an off-language reranker degrades the result rather than failing to
help. Selection is keyed on \emph{language}, not script: Vietnamese and
Indonesian are Latin-script and equally out of the English model's depth. Where
no model covers the language the stage is switched off entirely: two good
signals beat three when the third is arbitrary.

Shortlist size is
$k=\min(n,\,450,\,\max(40,\lceil 0.05n\rceil))$, except that a corpus of at most
80 provisions is graded in full, which makes retrieval a signal rather than a
gate --- our declared mitigation for economies whose retrieval parameters are
unmeasured.

\subsection{Indicator mapping}
\label{sec:map}

Each candidate is graded independently, and the prompt shows the target indicator
\emph{together with all of its siblings}, each with its own legal test. This
forces the explicit choice between adjacent indicators that retrieval cannot
make. The model returns structured fields --- operative rule, whether the target
is satisfied, a better sibling if the target is a mislabel, a graded legal-match
score, scope, and optionally a subsection --- and the contract is explicitly
symmetric between over-assignment and miss.

Two constraints define the anti-hallucination boundary. Non-English snippets are
reasoned over as written and never translated, because the verbatim column
\emph{is} the statute's text and a translation would make the citation false;
only the model's English rationale is generated. And the sole field the model may
contribute to the citation, a subsection reference, is kept only if it is
well-formed \emph{and} occurs literally in the snippet. Everything else in the
exported row is carried from extraction.

\subsection{Confidence and routing}
\label{sec:confidence}

Confidence is a weighted sum of four signals: the grader's legal-match judgement
(0.40), the fused retrieval score (0.25), snippet grounding (0.20), and scope
alignment (0.15). Grounding is a self-check --- whether the quoted snippet is
present in the source document --- and is the only signal computed without a
model. Two hard caps then apply: a scope-mismatched mapping on the
comprehensive-framework indicator is capped at 0.55, and a snippet sharing no
concept vocabulary with the pillar is capped at 0.45. Rows route to auto-accept,
human review, or quarantine. Indicators with no evidence receive an explicit
``no provision found'' row naming the portal searched; a blank is never emitted.

We state plainly that these six constants are hand-set. Fitting them needs
thousands of labelled provision-question pairs including hard negatives, and the
published answer key provides only final answers for three economies with no
near-miss negatives. Generating negatives ourselves would be grading our own
work. The design response is to surface all four signals and the verbatim
snippet on every row so a reviewer can reject a mapping directly, rather than to
present an uncalibrated number as a probability.

\subsection{The provenance harness}
\label{sec:provenance}

The pipeline's rule for law --- evidence is carried, never generated --- is
applied one level up to the paper. Experiments write one record per matrix point
carrying a content hash of its configuration and inputs; metrics collapse
records into cells, aggregating seed axes exactly once so no value reaches a
figure without its spread; and a checker walks this document, resolves every
cited value back to the records behind it, and fails the build on anything it
cannot ground. A condition matching two cells is an error rather than a
pick-the-first; a single-seed cell reports its spread as undefined rather than
zero; a failed run is recorded as failed rather than scored zero.

\subsection{Retrieval parameters are measured, not chosen}
\label{sec:sweep}

Parameters were swept against the panel's own answer key on a stratified corpus
of 383 laws and 36{,}590 provisions, scoring whether the specific provision the
panel named reaches the shortlist. Raising the shortlist from 40 to 300
candidates moved provision recall from 15/18 to 18/18. Fusion ablations at the
chosen budget: $\alpha=0.65$ gives 18/18, $\alpha=0.50$ gives 17/18, and pure
BM25 or pure dense each give 16/18 --- so both signals are load-bearing.
Removing the cross-encoder costs a target; using it alone costs three.

Two negative results are worth recording because both are tempting. A law-level
prefilter is \emph{uniformly worse} at every budget: a law whose title is about
something else still holds the one provision that answers the indicator, and a
law-level gate discards it before any provision is considered. And reserving
shortlist slots per law degenerates on a multi-hundred-law corpus into
breadth-without-depth. Measured end-to-end on production code, the parameter
change moved provision recall from 11/18 to 17/18, with Australia going from 1
of 5 cited provisions retrieved to 5 of 5 --- and the shortlist got
\emph{cleaner} as it got larger.

\subsection{An economy can disappear without an error}
\label{sec:ablation}

The experiments above are single-configuration measurements against a live
system. This one is controlled, reproducible offline, and re-derived from records
at build time. We cross six economies with two binary treatments --- tokenisation
(monolingual vs.\ script-aware) and query vocabulary (English vs.\ plus native
statutory terms) --- over \lnum{seeds_per_cell} seeded draws of a distractor pool,
indexing all economies' instruments together in one pooled index of
\lnum{n_docs} documents. Instruments are rendered as their own portal serves
them; Mongolia's are recovered into Cyrillic by matching the numeric instrument
identifier inside the source URL, a language-independent join.

We report \emph{reachability}: the share of the panel's cited instruments that
receive any non-zero lexical score at all. A zero here is not a ranking that went
badly; it is a document the query cannot see.

The structure of Table~\ref{tab:reach} is the finding. Script-aware tokenisation
is necessary and not sufficient: China stays at \lnum{reach_cn_script} once its
text is tokenisable, because an English query and a Chinese statute share no
tokens whatever the tokeniser does. Native vocabulary is likewise necessary and
not sufficient: under the monolingual tokeniser, adding Chinese phrases leaves
China at \lnum{reach_cn_ascii_nat}, because those phrases tokenise to nothing on
the way in.

The operational point is the mode, not the magnitude. Lexical scoring carries the
majority of the fused score, so an economy in this state contributes a uniform
zero to ranking, and the pipeline terminates normally reporting that no provision
was found --- which is a legitimate and common answer in a regulatory index.

\subsection{Widening the tokeniser is not free}

% ledger:strict
\claim{C2}
% ledger:endstrict

Under the monolingual rule the Cyrillic distractors tokenise to nothing: they
occupy the index but can never score. Making the tokeniser script-aware admits
them, which changes the document-frequency and length-normalisation statistics
BM25 shares across the collection \citep{robertson2009bm25}. Australia's
recall@10 falls from \lnum{r10_au_ascii} to \lnum{r10_au_script} although not one
byte of Australian tokenisation changed, while Malaysia rises from
\lnum{r10_my_ascii} to \lnum{r10_my_script}. Reachability, a per-document
property, is unchanged for every Latin economy; only rank-sensitive metrics move.
A team measuring only reachability would have concluded the change was free.

\subsection{Recognition quality}

On the one bundled scanned instrument with a human-checked transcript, RapidOCR
reaches 1.11\% character error at about 4\,s/page and PaddleOCR 0.00\% at
585\,s/page, against a 5\% requirement. We report a third number because it is
the more instructive one: MarkItDown initially measured 0.00\% error, and the
measurement was an artifact --- extracting nothing from an image-only PDF, it
fell back to reading the ground-truth transcript shipped beside the file and
compared itself against the answer key. Measured on a copy in a directory
without that sidecar, its true error is 95.56\%. The fix was to exclude
sidecar-reading engines from measurement and to run the benchmark on an isolated
copy.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Confidence is uncalibrated.} The four weights and two thresholds are
hand-set; they order rows by reliability but are not probabilities
(Section~\ref{sec:confidence}).

\textbf{There is no external baseline.} 18/18 target recall is good relative to
our own previous configuration and to nothing else. We built a scorer against the
panel's answer key to substitute, and it caught one of its own errors: Mongolia
initially scored 0 of 9 because the key names instruments in English while the
portal serves Mongolian, and no string comparison bridges that. The scorer was
wrong, not the pipeline.

\textbf{Coverage is bounded by discovery, not ranking.} Ten of thirty-seven cited
instruments are outside the catalogue because they are not statutes. Closing this
needs a separately enumerable index per instrument type, and most do not exist.

\textbf{Most of the framework is unscored.} Nine of sixty-one indicators have
codified legal tests; the remaining fifty-two run but have never been checked
for whether their definitions discriminate correctly.

\textbf{Single runs.} Every system-level number in Section~\ref{sec:coverage} is
one measurement against a live portal. Only Section~\ref{sec:ablation} is
reproducible offline.

\textbf{The controlled experiment measures the lexical floor.} It uses instrument
titles as the unit and reachability as the metric, not full provision text and
mapping F1. Provision-level corpora for three of the six economies do not yet
exist, and a live crawl is neither reproducible nor free. We report the layer we
can measure reproducibly and decline to report the one we cannot.

\section{Conclusion}

A legal-evidence pipeline that cannot be audited is one whose failures are
invisible, and the multilingual failures are the most invisible of all, because
they present as the same output a correct system produces on a jurisdiction with
no such law. We built a harness that discovers its own sources, refuses to let a
model author a citation, and refuses to publish a number it cannot trace to a
run; pointed it at its own retrieval layer; and found an economy that had been
silently absent under a tokenisation rule that was correct for every jurisdiction
it was developed against. The measurement is small and the discipline is not.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

The controlled experiment of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} runs offline with no
network, no credentials and no language model: both of its inputs --- the expert
panel's reference set and the harvested title catalogue --- are files in the
repository, so its records can be re-derived by anyone who clones it. The full
grid is \lnum{seeds_per_cell} seeds over the conditions of
Table~\ref{tab:reach}, and the code under test is imported from the shipped
system rather than reimplemented, because a benchmark that measures a copy of a
tokeniser measures the copy.

Every number in this paper that appears inside a verified region is a macro
resolved at compile time from those records, through the aggregation described in
Section~\ref{sec:provenance}; the document does not build if one stops resolving.
Generated tables and charts carry the hash of the metrics file they came from and
are re-checked against it at build time. Appendices~\ref{app:charts} and the
cell listing give the per-cell means, spreads and sample sizes behind every
figure.

The system-level runs of Section~\ref{sec:coverage} are \emph{not} reproducible
in this sense and we do not present them as though they were: they depend on
live government portals whose contents change, and each is a single measurement
on the date shown.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This system retrieves public law from official government portals, and the manner
of retrieval raises two issues we treat as design constraints rather than
implementation details.

\textbf{Collection.} Crawling is rate-limited per host and honours
\texttt{robots.txt}, with an unreachable directive treated as denial rather than
permission --- a server that cannot state its rules has not granted any. Where a
portal declares that its documents are not to be downloaded we stop and use a
different official source that publishes the same instruments, accepting the
coverage cost (Section~\ref{sec:acquire}). One narrow, named exception relaxes
certificate validation for two hosts serving public statutes with expired
certificates; no credential is ever sent, and every fetched body is hashed so a
substituted document is detectable after the fact.

\textbf{Use.} The output feeds a regulatory index that informs policy, so a wrong
citation is not a benign error. This motivates the architecture rather than
sitting beside it: the model may not author a citation, every row carries the
verbatim source text and all four confidence signals, and rows below threshold
are routed to human review rather than published. We state explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:confidence} that the confidence score is uncalibrated and
therefore orders rows by reliability without being a probability --- presenting
it as one would invite exactly the misplaced trust the system is built to avoid.

\section{Portal mechanisms}
\label{app:portals}

Each portal required a different mechanism, and in every case the naive approach
returned a plausible empty result rather than an error. Dates are the days the
behaviour was verified against the live site.

\paragraph{Malaysia --- an encrypted catalogue.} The legislation portal answers
its catalogue endpoint with \texttt{\{"encrypted": true, "data": "\ldots"\}}. The
payload is AES-256-GCM laid out as a 12-byte IV, a 16-byte tag, then ciphertext,
and the key is a 64-hex-character constant the page publishes in its own source
so that its client-side JavaScript can decrypt it. We do exactly what that script
does. This is a transport wrapper rather than an access control --- the plaintext
is the same public catalogue of Malaysian legislation --- but a client that does
not implement it reads \emph{zero} Acts and raises nothing. Two catalogues are
enumerated, principal and amendment, because the principal one carries dated
reprints into which a later amendment has not yet been folded.

\paragraph{Singapore --- a pagination parameter that is ignored.} The browse
listing accepts a page parameter and disregards it: pages one, two and three
return byte-identical HTML at every page size, with or without the AJAX header
(verified 2026-08-01). The index is therefore not walkable. What the portal does
honour is sort order, so asking for the same list ascending and then descending
returns its first and last windows, and their union is the whole index whenever
the total is at most twice the page size. Four windows over two sort keys cover
the 524 current Acts. They do \emph{not} cover the 5{,}843 subsidiary
instruments, and the shortfall is logged as incomplete rather than passed over in
silence. The same portal throttles a burst by answering HTTP 202 with an empty
body rather than 429, so a naive client reads success and an empty page.

\paragraph{Australia --- multi-volume compilations.} The register exposes an
OData API whose filter matches against the Act \emph{title} only, so descriptive
obligation phrases can never hit and are suppressed for this portal. Large Acts
are published as multiple volumes, and for those the single-file PDF URL returns
404 --- the volume number must be appended. Before this was handled the 404 was
silent: the fetch failed, the pipeline fell back to the JavaScript landing page,
and the Act contributed one junk provision instead of roughly eight hundred. It
struck exactly the largest Acts, which is where retention and government-access
provisions live. Volumes must also be extracted and segmented \emph{separately}
before merging: concatenating them first makes the second volume's opening body
look like a table of contents, which deleted an entire data-retention Part.

\paragraph{India --- one record per section.} India Code exposes a DSpace API
that publishes each \emph{section} as its own item, so provision boundaries are
the publisher's rather than ours and no OCR or segmentation heuristic is
involved. Two constraints are non-obvious. The API silently caps a page at 100
items however many are requested, while reporting a page count computed as though
it had returned the larger number --- so a client that trusts it loses most of a
long Act with no error. And queries must be quoted phrases: the unquoted form of
a cross-border data query returns the Commercial Courts Act, a highways rule and
a police manual, with the actual data-protection statute absent.

\paragraph{Mongolia --- a body that is not in the page.} The detail page injects
its content client-side; the roughly twelve thousand Cyrillic characters in the
served shell are navigation, a modal and a sector index. The document is reached
through the export endpoint the page's own toolbar calls, which returns
Word-flavoured HTML --- so no OCR and no binary parse. Titles are matched
per-word rather than as phrases, because Mongolian word order differs from the
answer key's and the portal's own filenames contain missing spaces; per-word
matching also carries agglutination in the right direction, since a stem reaches
its declined forms. One detail decided whether the economy worked at all:
converting block-level elements to newlines \emph{before} stripping tags. Without
it the law arrives as 34{,}196 characters of correct Mongolian on a single line,
from which a line-anchored article pattern finds nothing --- and the run
completes, extracts, maps and reports no provision found.

\paragraph{China --- where we stop.} The national law database's own API returns
a permission block containing \texttt{download:\,0}, and the document path
resolves through a signed reader on an internal host. That is the operator
stating the documents are not to be downloaded, so we do not engineer around it.
The cyberspace administration's portal publishes the same national laws as
ordinary server-rendered pages, and that is the lane used. The cost is real: the
provincial tier remains reachable but is the wrong tier for national indicators.

\section{Worked segmentation and retrieval examples}
\label{app:cases}

Three instruments, one per script, walked through the same code the controlled
experiment measures. The aggregate result of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} says an
economy is unreachable; these show why, token by token. The Singaporean case is
the control --- Latin script throughout, so no condition changes anything.

Native titles are reproduced as images. This is not a stylistic choice: the TeX
installation used for this build has no CJK package and no Cyrillic encoding, and
transliteration is not available to us, because the pipeline's own rule is that
statutory text is carried and never rewritten. What appears below is the
statute's own text rather than a romanisation of it. Non-Latin tokens are counted
rather than printed for the same reason, and counted separately from Latin ones
so that a mixed-script query shows which half is doing the work.
